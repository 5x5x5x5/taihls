# How to Learn from This Book

Reading a coding and science textbook like a novel — scrolling past code cells without pausing — feels easy, but almost nothing sticks a week later. Real learning happens in the tiny pause between *guessing* what will happen and *seeing* what actually happens.

Every chapter in **TAIHLS** follows a five-step classroom habit called **PRIMM** (*Predict, Run, Investigate, Modify, Make*):

```{mermaid}
flowchart LR
    P["1. Predict<br/>Guess what the code<br/>will output"] --> R["2. Run<br/>Execute the cell<br/>and check"]
    R --> I["3. Investigate<br/>Understand why<br/>it happened"]
    I --> M["4. Modify<br/>Change one number<br/>and compare"]
    M --> K["5. Make<br/>Try a tiny new<br/>problem yourself"]
```

## Five simple habits for a great semester

```{admonition} Habit 1 — Pause for 15 seconds at every "Predict before you run" box
:class: tip
Commit to an answer in your head (or whisper it to a classmate) *before* clicking Run. Getting a prediction **wrong** is actually the fastest way to learn — surprise locks a concept into memory far better than nodding along!
```

- **Habit 2 — Read code for its story, not its punctuation.** Don't try to memorize Python commands. Ask: *What data goes in? What simple step happens inside the loop? What answer comes out?*
- **Habit 3 — Tinker on purpose.** Once a cell runs, change one number — make a drug kill rate smaller, raise a classifier's threshold, shrink a patient group — and watch the picture change.
- **Habit 4 — Try the exercises before opening the solution.** Spending even three minutes thinking before clicking the dropdown is where real problem-solving skill grows.
- **Habit 5 — Use "Going Deeper" boxes when you're curious.** Every chapter's main text uses high-school algebra and plain English. Collapsible **Going Deeper** boxes are 100% optional bonuses for students who want extra math or technical detail.

## Using AI chatbots smartly while studying

You probably use AI assistants in daily life. In this class, treat an AI chatbot like a **coach**, never a copy machine:

::::{tab-set}

:::{tab-item} Helpful ways to use an AI coach
- *"Walk me through line 6 of this Python loop using just 3 numbers."*
- *"Here is my guess for Exercise 2.1 — tell me if my logic has a gap without giving away the final code."*
- *"Give me a real-world analogy for why standardization matters."*
:::

:::{tab-item} Shortcuts that hurt your learning
- Pasting an exercise straight into a chatbot and copying the answer without understanding it.
- Skipping the **Predict** box because a chatbot already explained the answer.
- Trusting health facts or study citations from a chatbot without verifying them (we'll see why in Unit 4!).
:::

::::
