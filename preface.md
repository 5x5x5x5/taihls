# Preface — Why This Book Exists

In October 2024, the Nobel Prize in Chemistry honored scientists who taught artificial intelligence to predict the three-dimensional shapes of proteins and design brand-new ones from scratch [@jumper2021alphafold; @watson2023rfdiffusion]. Soon after, newer AI systems began modeling how proteins lock onto DNA and medicines [@abramson2024alphafold3], reading vast stretches of the human genome [@avsec2026alphagenome; @brixi2026evo2], and assisting radiologists in massive randomized breast-cancer screening trials [@lang2023masai; @lang2026masai].

At the very same time, careful researchers uncovered sobering mistakes: a widely used hospital algorithm that underestimated the medical needs of Black patients because it predicted healthcare *spending* instead of *illness* [@obermeyer2019bias]; chest X-ray classifiers that looked brilliant in the lab only because they memorized hospital scanner tags instead of lung disease [@zech2018pneumonia; @degrave2021covid]; and chatbots that invent completely fake medical studies in a smooth, confident tone.

Whether you plan to become a doctor, a biologist, a software engineer, a public-health policymaker, or simply an informed patient, understanding how health AI works — and when to question it — is one of the most important skills of your generation.

## Our promise to high-school and intro-college learners

Most introductions to AI fall into one of two traps: either they throw a wall of university calculus and matrix math at you on page 3, or they stay so vague that you never learn how the machines actually work.

**TAIHLS** takes a third path built specifically for a **one-semester advanced high-school class**:

1. **One big idea per chapter.** Each of our 28 short chapters focuses on a single core concept so you never feel buried under a firehose of jargon.
2. **Everyday story → hand-worked numbers → short Python.** Before you ever run a program, we work through a tiny 3-number example by hand in plain English. Every technical and medical word is defined the first time it appears and collected in the [Glossary](back/glossary.md).
3. **High ceiling, welcoming floor.** You only need high-school algebra and curiosity (Chapter 0.1 refreshes all the Python basics!). If you're hungry for more math or deeper technical details, optional collapsible **"Going Deeper"** boxes are waiting right on the page without slowing anyone else down.

Welcome to the course. Let's open the hood together.
